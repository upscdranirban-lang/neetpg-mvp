NEET-PG 2026 Round 1 Trends: Which Branches Went Deeper Than 2025 (Real MCC Data)

2026-11-13

The short version: we extracted every row of MCC's 2026 Round 1 result (28,723 allotments across 1,735 pages) and compared it with the 2025 Round 1 result. Three things stand out: more seats were filled overall, Anaesthesiology, General Surgery, Orthopaedics and Pathology reached much deeper down the rank list, and General Medicine, Paediatrics, OB-GYN and Dermatology barely moved. All numbers below come straight from the official PDFs, not from third-party sites.

Where the data comes from (and how we checked it)

The source is MCC's Round 1 allotment result for 2026. We extracted it twice, independently: once from the provisional result dated 23 October and once from the final result dated 24 October. The two extractions matched exactly — same 28,723 rows, and not a single closing rank differing. The 2025 comparison uses our existing extraction of MCC's 2025 Round 1 result (26,889 rows). Both are now in the Rank Predictor and College Predictor as 2026 Round 1 and 2025 Round 1.

Trend 1: more seats were filled in Round 1

Counting the allotments the way our predictor does (PwD-reserved seats and Armed Forces service-bond institutes are excluded, so the totals are slightly lower than the raw row count), Round 1 filled about 28,500 seats in 2026 versus about 26,700 in 2025 — roughly 6.6% more. The growth was not even across categories:

Trend 2: some branches reached far deeper than last year

The chart and table show the Open-category closing rank in Round 1 — the last Open rank that received that branch (MD/MS track, branches with at least 150 allotments in both years). A higher number means Round 1 reached further down the merit list.

Anaesthesiology, General Surgery, Orthopaedics and Pathology all now close at around 1.59–1.60 lakh in Round 1. That is what a branch looks like when many seats are still available to candidates deep in the list. The four branches that stayed near 1.33 lakh (General Medicine, Paediatrics, OB-GYN, Dermatology) tell a different story: the last Open candidates getting those branches in Round 1 were at essentially the same rank as last year.

Trend 3: the DNB and Diploma tracks followed the same pattern

  • Anaesthesiology (DNB): Open closing rank 32,058 → 36,391
  • Anaesthesiology (Diploma): 47,692 → 59,746
  • Ophthalmology (Diploma): 45,926 → 60,000
  • General Medicine (DNB): 12,999 → 12,413 (essentially flat)

The direction matches the MD/MS track: procedure and support branches stretch further; General Medicine holds.

What this means for you

  • Do not assume last year's cutoffs still apply. For Anaesthesiology, Surgery, Orthopaedics and Pathology the 2025 Round 1 numbers now understate how far down the list a seat can go.
  • For General Medicine, Paediatrics, OB-GYN and Dermatology, 2025 Round 1 remains a fair guide to the overall depth of Round 1.
  • Later rounds move these numbers. Round 1 closing ranks are a floor for how deep a branch ultimately goes, not a final cutoff.

To see where your rank sits, pick 2026 Round 1 in the Rank Predictor or College Predictor. Both show historical closing ranks only; they are not a promise of admission. Also see our guide on whether to report, upgrade or wait, and beware of guaranteed-seat scam calls.

Limits you should know about

  • "Closing rank" here is the last rank allotted in Round 1. A single high-rank candidate can stretch it, so we limited the table to branches with 150+ allotments in both years.
  • A few branches are named differently across years (for example Radiology / Radio-Diagnosis), so we left them out rather than guess a match.
  • PwD-reserved allotments and Armed Forces service-bond institutes are excluded, as on the predictor.
  • Cohorts differ year to year; a rank is not the same as a percentile.

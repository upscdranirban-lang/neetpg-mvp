NEET-PG 2026 Round 1 Result Is Out: Report, Upgrade or Wait? Dates, Documents and Real 2025 Data

2026-10-24

The short version: MCC published the Round 1 final allotment result on 24 October 2026. If you were allotted a seat, the reporting window runs 26 October to 2 November, and MCC verifies joined-candidate data on 3 November. Round 2 is tentatively scheduled to begin on 6 November. Everything below is meant to help you use the next eight days well -- not to tell you what to do with your seat, because that is your call.

Your document checklist (start collecting today)

Reporting day is not the day to discover a missing certificate. The list below is what the major counselling explainers agree on; your allotted institute's own list always prevails, so treat this as a starting point and check your allotment letter.

  • NEET-PG admit card and result scorecard
  • The Round 1 seat allotment letter (download it and keep a printout)
  • MBBS (or BDS) degree or provisional certificate, plus mark sheets of all professional exams
  • Internship completion certificate
  • NMC or State Medical Council registration certificate
  • Date-of-birth proof and a valid photo ID, plus passport-size photographs
  • Category, EWS or disability certificate, if you are using that reservation
  • Anything seat-specific: domicile, NRI or sponsorship papers, service-bond or fee documents, fitness certificate

Carry originals and photocopies. If a certificate is missing, ask your university or council now -- those offices are not fast.

At top government colleges, the picture is very different

General Medicine, Open category, 2025 -- a few well-known colleges, Round 1 versus Round 3:

Three things stand out, and none of them is a promise:

  • Top-college seats mostly go in Round 1. Maulana Azad had no Open-category General Medicine allotment recorded in Round 3 at all.
  • Some seats do reopen, and the ranks can move a lot. Madras Medical College's Round 1 General Medicine allotments ended at rank 291; its Round 3 allotments reached rank 799. For a few candidates, waiting changed the outcome.
  • The reopened seats are few. Round 3 had one seat at UCMS and two at Madras Medical College. Planning around two or three possible seats is a gamble, whatever your rank.

Treat this as a pattern from one year, in one category, with small seat counts at the college level. Seat matrices, applicant numbers and cutoffs change every cycle, so 2025 describes a shape, not a forecast.

Three questions to answer before 26 October

  1. Would I be glad to hold this seat? Check the college, the specialty, the fee, any service bond and the stipend -- not just the rank attached to it.
  2. What do my allotment letter and MCC's Information Bulletin say? Rules on upgrading, exiting a seat, security deposits and what happens if you don't report differ by quota and seat type, and they are the only authority that counts. Read them before you decide, not after.
  3. Are my documents ready for the whole window? Eight days sounds long until a certificate needs a signature in another city.
